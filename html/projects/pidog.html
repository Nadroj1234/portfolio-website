PiDog AI Project

For this project, I built and programmed a PiDog V2 robotic dog using a Raspberry Pi 4 Model B. I worked on getting the robot to move, respond to voice commands, play sounds, and connect to an AI model.

AI

I connected the PiDog to Ollama so that it could use an AI model to understand and respond to voice commands. The Raspberry Pi connects to Ollama running on another computer on the same network.

I also worked with speech recognition and text-to-speech so that the PiDog could listen for commands and respond with its voice.

Movement

I programmed the PiDog to respond to different commands and actions. I also worked on controlling the robot with a physical game controller, using the joysticks and buttons to control its movement.

Getting the controller working correctly required testing the controller inputs and adjusting how the robot responded when the joysticks were moved and released.

Camera

I worked on getting the PiDog's camera working with the Raspberry Pi. This involved troubleshooting the camera connection, Raspberry Pi camera software, and the camera sensor.

Audio

I also worked on the PiDog's audio system. I tested the built-in sounds and added my own sound files so the robot could play different sounds for actions such as barking and howling.

What I Learned

This project has helped me learn how hardware and software work together. I have worked with Python, Raspberry Pi hardware, controllers, cameras, audio devices, APIs, speech recognition, text-to-speech, and AI models.

One of the biggest parts of the project has been troubleshooting. I have had to diagnose problems with the network connection, camera, controller, audio playback, and AI connection and then test different solutions.

Future Plans

I plan to continue improving the PiDog so that it can use its sensors, camera, movement, audio, and AI together to behave more independently.
